The best **Friendsgiving games** are easy to explain in under a minute, work for mixed groups of 4 to 20 people, and need nothing but a phone or some paper. Word games like Taboo, charades-style Heads Up, and social deduction games like Imposter are the crowd favorites because everyone can jump in, even mid-food-coma.

[Friendsgiving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friendsgiving) is the Thanksgiving-style feast you share with friends instead of (or on top of) family. It usually happens the weekend before Thanksgiving, which falls on **Thursday, November 26 in 2026**. The food is the main event, but the hours after dinner are where the best memories happen. That is where a good game comes in.

Below are 15 games that work for any Friendsgiving crowd, a quick comparison table, a free Thanksgiving word list, and picks for every kind of crowd.

## The 15 best Friendsgiving games

### 1. Tadado: Forbidden Words

**Players:** 4+ in two teams · **Time:** 15–30 min · **Needs:** a phone

[Tadado](https://tadado.app/en) is a free party game app, and its [Forbidden Words](/en/modes/forbidden-words) mode is the Taboo-style game every group loves. One player describes a word to their team without saying it or any of the forbidden words listed under it. Describe "Turkey" without saying bird, gobble, or Thanksgiving and watch the chaos unfold.

**How to play:**

1. Split into two teams and pick a describer.
2. The describer gets 60 seconds to get through as many cards as possible.
3. Each correct guess scores a point; saying a forbidden word costs one.
4. Rotate describers and teams until everyone has had a turn.

**Why it works at Friendsgiving:** It is loud, fast, and everyone already knows the rules. In Tadado, Forbidden Words adds score multipliers from ×2 to ×5, so a losing team can always come back.

### 2. Heads Up

**Players:** 3+ · **Time:** 10–20 min · **Needs:** a phone

One player holds the phone to their forehead while everyone else shouts clues, acts things out, or hums. Tilt down for a correct guess, up to pass.

**How to play:**

1. Pick a deck and start the timer.
2. The guesser holds the phone up facing the group.
3. Everyone gives clues until time runs out.
4. Pass the phone and compare scores.

**Why it works:** Perfect for the post-dinner slump. Nobody has to stand up for long, and even quiet guests end up yelling clues. Tadado's [Heads Up](/en/modes/heads-up) mode works offline, so a bad Wi-Fi signal will not stop the game.

### 3. Imposter

**Players:** 4–12 · **Time:** 15–30 min · **Needs:** a phone

Everyone gets the same secret word except one player: the imposter. Players take turns giving a one-word clue, then vote on who is faking it.

**How to play:**

1. Pass the phone around so each player privately sees their word (or "You're the Imposter").
2. Go around the table, each player saying one word related to the secret word.
3. Discuss, accuse, and vote.
4. If the group catches the imposter, they win. If not, the imposter does.

**Why it works:** It turns the dinner table into a courtroom. The imposter has to bluff using only the clues they have heard, and the debates are often funnier than the game. Try it in Tadado's [Imposter](/en/modes/imposter) mode.

### 4. Classic Charades

**Players:** 4+ · **Time:** 20–40 min · **Needs:** nothing

The party game everyone's grandma knows. Act out a movie, book, or phrase without speaking while your team guesses. [Charades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charades) has been a parlor favorite for centuries for a reason.

**Friendsgiving twist:** Only act out Thanksgiving-themed prompts: "carving the turkey," "watching the parade," "unbuttoning your jeans after pie."

### 5. Fishbowl (The Hat Game)

**Players:** 6+ in two teams · **Time:** 30–45 min · **Needs:** paper slips, pens, a bowl

Everyone writes 3–5 names or phrases on slips and drops them in a bowl. The same slips are used across three rounds:

1. Round 1: Describe it with any words (Taboo-style).
2. Round 2: Use only one word.
3. Round 3: Act it out with no words at all.

**Why it works:** Inside jokes build with every round. By round three, a single gesture can make the whole room cry laughing.

### 6. Mafia / Werewolf

**Players:** 6–20 · **Time:** 20–40 min · **Needs:** nothing (a narrator helps)

A hidden group of "mafia" secretly eliminates players each night, while townspeople try to figure out who they are during the day. [Mafia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mafia_(party_game)) is one of the best games for large Friendsgiving groups because more players means more drama.

**Tip:** If you love the bluffing but want shorter rounds, Imposter (#3) delivers the same tension in under 5 minutes per round.

### 7. Two Truths and a Lie

**Players:** 3+ · **Time:** 15–20 min · **Needs:** nothing

Each person shares three statements about themselves. Two are true, one is a lie. Everyone guesses the lie.

**Why it works:** Friendsgiving often mixes friend groups, roommates, and plus-ones. This is the easiest icebreaker to get everyone talking.

### 8. Thankful Hot Seat

**Players:** 4+ · **Time:** 15 min · **Needs:** nothing

One person sits in the "hot seat" while everyone else shares one thing they are thankful for about them. Rotate until everyone has had a turn.

**Why it works:** It is the wholesome moment of the night, and it keeps the "thanks" in Friendsgiving without a formal speech.

### 9. Most Likely To

**Players:** 5+ · **Time:** 15–20 min · **Needs:** nothing

Someone reads a prompt like "Who's most likely to eat a whole pie alone?" On the count of three, everyone points at a person. Most votes takes the title.

**Thanksgiving prompts:** most likely to burn the turkey, fall asleep during the football game, or show up with store-bought sides and claim they are homemade.

### 10. Telephone Pictionary

**Players:** 4–12 · **Time:** 20 min · **Needs:** paper and pens

Each player writes a phrase, passes it on, and the next person draws it. The next player writes what they think the drawing is, and so on. At the end, reveal how "Pilgrims landing at Plymouth Rock" became "a boat full of potatoes."

### 11. Thanksgiving Would You Rather

**Players:** any · **Time:** 10–20 min · **Needs:** nothing

Keep it going while people are still at the table. Examples:

- Would you rather only eat stuffing for a year or never eat pie again?
- Would you rather host Thanksgiving for 30 people or go to 5 dinners in one day?
- Would you rather have no gravy or no cranberry sauce?

### 12. Turkey Trivia

**Players:** 4+ in teams · **Time:** 20–30 min · **Needs:** a phone or printout

Prepare 15–20 questions about Thanksgiving history, food, and pop culture, or make it personal with questions about the group. Winning team skips dish duty.

### 13. Thanksgiving Alphabet

**Players:** 3+ · **Time:** 10 min · **Needs:** nothing

Go around the circle naming something Thanksgiving-related for each letter of the alphabet: A for apple pie, B for butter, C for cranberry... Hesitate for more than five seconds and you are out.

### 14. Blind Pie Taste Test

**Players:** 4+ · **Time:** 15 min · **Needs:** desserts and a blindfold

Blindfold a volunteer and have them guess desserts, sides, or leftovers by taste alone. It is a natural fit for a potluck where everyone brought something different.

### 15. Song Association

**Players:** 3+ · **Time:** 10–15 min · **Needs:** nothing

Someone calls out a word ("turkey," "thankful," "pie") and the next player has five seconds to sing a lyric that contains it. Miss it and you are out. Great for the final stretch of the night.

## 6 tips for hosting a Friendsgiving game night

1. **Start with a low-effort game.** Right after dinner, play something seated like Heads Up or Would You Rather. Save the high-energy games for later.
2. **Keep rule explanations under a minute.** If you cannot explain it fast, people lose interest. Phone-based games handle the rules and timers for you.
3. **Mix teams on purpose.** Pair people who do not know each other well. It is the easiest way to blend friend groups.
4. **Have one game that works for any group size.** Guests arrive late and leave early. Charades and Heads Up adapt instantly.
5. **Plan for bad Wi-Fi.** Pick games that work offline so a weak signal never kills the mood.
6. **Add a small prize.** The winning team takes home the leftover pie or skips dish duty. Stakes make everything funnier.

## Friendsgiving games FAQ

### What games do you play at Friendsgiving?

The most popular Friendsgiving games are word and guessing games like Taboo, Heads Up, and Charades, plus social games like Imposter, Mafia, and Two Truths and a Lie. They are easy to learn and work for groups of any size.

### What are good Friendsgiving games for adults?

Imposter, Most Likely To, Fishbowl, and Song Association are great adult picks. They reward bluffing, quick thinking, and knowing your friends a little too well.

### What games work for large Thanksgiving groups?

Fishbowl, Mafia, and team-based Taboo scale best to 10–20 players. Split into two teams and rotate turns so nobody sits out for long.

### What can I play after Thanksgiving dinner without any supplies?

Charades, Two Truths and a Lie, Would You Rather, Most Likely To, Thanksgiving Alphabet, and Song Association need nothing at all. Phone games like Tadado need only one phone for the whole group.
